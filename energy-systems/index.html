About This Model

This qualitative tracker is an instructional interpretation of research [1]. The three moving bars visualize broad patterns of overlap: rapid phosphagen support, rapid glycolytic activation, and increasing oxidative support. Their drawing coordinates and time constants are chosen for illustration, not fitted to research data. It is not a validated equation. It describes pathway roles at selected moments; it does not report ATP production rates, percentages, or total energy across the bout. Exercise mode, training, pacing, prior activity, and measurement method affect the balance. Glycolysis and oxidative metabolism are connected processes, not independent switches.

View research evidence and references

Published findings below are whole-bout estimates under specific study conditions. They are not values assigned to the activities above.

30-second maximal cycling [2]

Smith and Hill studied six men completing modified Wingate tests. Estimated whole-bout contributions were phosphagen 28%, glycolytic 56%, and aerobic 16%. Phosphagen power peaked in the first 5 seconds, glycolytic power at 10–15 seconds, and aerobic power in the final 5 seconds. These are study findings, not universal crossover times.

Simulated running events [3]

Spencer and Gastin studied 20 highly trained athletes. Estimated whole-bout aerobic contributions were 29% for 200 m, 43% for 400 m, 66% for 800 m, and 84% for 1500 m. A pathway's contribution at one moment differs from its accumulated contribution across the whole activity.

  1. Baker, McCormick & Robergs (2010). Interaction among Skeletal Muscle Metabolic Energy Systems during Intense Exercise. Review supporting overlap, rapid onset responses, and capacity limits.
  2. Smith & Hill (1991). Contribution of energy systems during a Wingate power test. DOI: 10.1136/bjsm.25.4.196.
  3. Spencer & Gastin (2001). Energy system contribution during 200- to 1500-m running in highly trained athletes. DOI: 10.1097/00005768-200101000-00024.
