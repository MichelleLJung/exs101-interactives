Client Investigation

Start Here

Work as an exercise science team. Find out what shapes your fictional client’s physical activity, explain their experiences using course concepts, and test and revise a recommendation that fits their circumstances.

Prepare your group

  • Use one device and confirm your assigned client.
  • Share or rotate roles. The interviewer asks questions, the concept investigator checks the references, the evidence analyst tracks what the client says, and the critical reviewer tests the group’s reasoning. Combine roles as needed.
  • Open the three reference pages from your Canvas module and keep them handy.

    Keep all examples fictional. Do not enter names, contact information, personal experiences or health details. Live interview and review text goes to OpenAI. Standard abuse-monitoring logs can retain content for up to 30 days, with exceptions under provider policy. This activity keeps your group work on the open page and does not save it to a database. Your instructor sets an interview limit and one separate review. Teammate roleplay uses no AI and follows the same steps.

    Open the investigation in a new tab before starting if you need more space.

    Investigate

    Ask open-ended questions. Gather evidence about both barriers and strengths before proposing a strategy.

    Open-ended question examples
    • What matters most to you about moving more, if anything?
    • What activity have you enjoyed or avoided in the past? What made it feel that way?
    • What would you feel confident doing this week?
    • What gets in the way on a typical day? How do the people and places around you affect that?
    • What people, places or routines could help you get started?

      Interpret

      Explain three plausible concepts using specific client evidence. Consider whether the issue is willingness, confidence, opportunity or another influence. Short notes are enough.

      View interview transcript

      Recommend

      Choose a strategy that makes sense for this person. Be specific enough for the client to tell you what would and would not work.

      Reassess

      Present your recommendation and ask what would feel realistic or difficult. Treat disagreement as evidence, then revise.

      Review

      Check each criterion using Demonstrated, Developing or Not Yet Evident. Agree on one meaningful next step. If you request an AI review, compare its feedback with your own evidence and the Canvas references.

      • We investigated both strengths and barriers before offering advice.
      • We used the psychological concepts accurately.
      • We connected specific client evidence to each interpretation.
      • Our strategy and first step fit the client’s priorities and circumstances.
      • Our revision responds to something the client actually said.

      Activate live access to request a review, or self-review using the criteria above.

      Debrief

      Prepare to share one major influence on your client’s behavior, the evidence behind your interpretation, your strategy, and how and why it changed. You can share verbally or refer to your notes.
