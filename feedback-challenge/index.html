The Feedback Challenge

Prepare → Throw → Interpret → Submit

Plan: 10 minutes preparation, 30 minutes maximum for data collection, then reflection and submission. Work in a team of three. Use a soft ball or beanbag.

Complete the preparation questions and prediction to unlock the throwing rounds.

1. Prepare in Class

Goal: Explore how feedback helps you adjust a throw when you cannot see or locate the target.

Teams of three: Learner throws; coach provides the assigned feedback; recorder tracks outcomes and keeps the area clear. Rotate roles so everyone throws. Each student completes their own reflection and PDF. Allow approximately 30 minutes for the full team’s data collection.

Feedback vocabulary

KR, knowledge of results: information about the outcome, such as where the ball landed.

KP, knowledge of performance: information about the movement, such as release or arm path.

Qualitative: describes direction or quality without a measurement.

Quantitative: includes a number or measured amount.

KR and KP describe what feedback is about. Qualitative and quantitative describe how specific it is.

How the hidden-target experiment works

  1. The coach places the floor target where the learner cannot see it. The recorder observes landing positions, records outcomes, and monitors safety. The learner must not know its direction or distance.
  2. The learner stands behind the marked line, facing a consistent direction. Use the non-dominant hand.
  3. Close or cover eyes only while stationary and throwing. The recorder ensures the throwing area is clear.
  4. After each throw, the coach gives only the feedback allowed for that round. The learner may adjust the next throw.
  5. Before each new round, move the target to a new hidden position. Keep the throwing line fixed. Rotate learner, coach, and recorder roles after each learner completes all three conditions. Each person completes 15 throws. Use the same three target locations for all learners, concealed throughout, and alternate the condition order between teams when practical.

Safety: Use soft objects, throw underhand, keep bystanders outside the throwing zone, and reopen eyes before walking or retrieving equipment. The recorder retrieves balls after the learner has finished throwing and is safely oriented. If closing eyes is unsafe or inaccessible, use a visual barrier that hides the target while allowing safe orientation, or complete the observer/coach role.

Quick concept check

A coach says, “Your throw landed two feet to the right.” What kind of feedback is this?

A coach says, “Your arm crossed your body at release.” What kind of information is this?

2. Qualitative KR

Outcome feedback, no numbers. After each throw, the coach reports only direction: “short,” “long,” “left,” “right,” or a combination. If the ball lands in the target, say “hit.”

The learner cannot see the target or landing location. Start with a newly hidden target. The recorder notes the result; the coach gives the permitted feedback. Complete 5 throws per learner. Each learner records their own five results while teammates coach and record.

3. Quantitative KR

Outcome feedback with numbers. The coach describes direction and approximate distance: “two feet short,” “one foot right,” or “hit.” Use a tape measure or marked floor grid; report distances in the same unit throughout. Estimates should be labeled approximate.

Move the target to a new hidden location. Complete 5 throws per learner. Each learner records their own five results while teammates coach and record. Do not give movement advice.

4. KR + KP

Outcome plus movement feedback. After each throw, the coach gives a brief KR statement (where it landed) and one KP cue (what the throwing movement looked like).

KR example

“The ball landed short and left.”

KP example

“Your release was very low.”

Only report movements you actually observed. Avoid guessing what caused the miss. One cue at a time. The KR + KP condition does not isolate KP.

Move the target again. Complete 5 throws per learner. Each learner records their own five results while teammates coach and record. This is a combined-feedback condition, not a pure KP comparison. Different hidden target locations and repeated practice limit causal conclusions.

5. Reflect & Submit

Use your own results. A higher hit count does not prove one feedback type produces better long-term motor learning: the target changes, the order matters, and you have only a few trials. This is an exploratory investigation of immediate adjustments, not a retention test.
