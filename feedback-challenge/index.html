EXS101 · The Feedback Challenge

Materials: Soft ball, floor target, marked throwing line, tape measure or marked floor grid.
Time: About 10 minutes to prepare and up to 30 minutes to collect data, then reflect.

Complete the preparation questions and prediction to unlock the throwing rounds.

1. Prepare in Class

Goal: Explore how feedback helps you adjust a throw when you cannot see or locate the target.

Roles: Learner throws; coach gives feedback; recorder tracks results and keeps the area clear. Rotate after each learner completes all three rounds. Each person submits their own data, reflection, and PDF.

Feedback vocabulary

KR, knowledge of results: information about the outcome, such as where the ball landed.

KP, knowledge of performance: information about the movement, such as release or arm path.

Qualitative: describes direction or quality without a measurement.

Quantitative: includes a number or measured amount.

KR and KP describe what feedback is about. Qualitative and quantitative describe how specific it is.

How the hidden-target experiment works

  1. The coach sets a hidden target. Keep its direction and distance unknown to the learner.
  2. Stand at the fixed throwing line. Throw underhand with your non-dominant hand; close or cover your eyes only while stationary.
  3. The coach gives only the round’s assigned feedback. The recorder records the outcome and feedback, retrieves the ball, and hands it directly to the learner. Never throw the ball back to someone.
  4. Complete five throws, then move the target to the next hidden location. Complete all three rounds before rotating roles: 15 throws per learner.

Safety: Keep the throwing area clear. Open your eyes before moving. If closing your eyes is unsafe or inaccessible, use a barrier that hides the target or take a coach/recorder role.

Use the same three hidden target locations for each learner. Follow your instructor’s assigned condition order.

Quick concept check

A coach says, “Your throw landed two feet to the right.” What kind of feedback is this?

A coach says, “Your arm crossed your body at release.” What kind of information is this?

2. Qualitative KR

Outcome feedback, no numbers. After each throw, the coach reports only direction: “short,” “long,” “left,” “right,” or a combination. If the ball lands in the target, say “hit.”

Keep the target and landing location hidden. Complete 5 throws. The recorder tracks your results; enter them below.

3. Quantitative KR

Outcome feedback with numbers. The coach describes direction and approximate distance: “two feet short,” “one foot right,” or “hit.” Use a tape measure or marked floor grid; report distances in the same unit throughout. Estimates should be labeled approximate.

Move the target to a new hidden location. Complete 5 throws and enter your results. Do not give movement advice.

4. KR + KP

Outcome plus movement feedback. After each throw, the coach gives a brief KR statement (where it landed) and one KP cue (what the throwing movement looked like).

KR example

“The ball landed short and left.”

KP example

“Your release was very low.”

Describe one observed movement; do not guess what caused a miss.

Move the target again. Complete 5 throws and enter your results. This round combines KR and KP; it does not test KP alone.

5. Reflect & Submit

Compare your immediate adjustments. A few trials, changing targets, and practice order limit your conclusions. More hits do not establish better long-term learning; this is not a retention test.
