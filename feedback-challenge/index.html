EXS101 · The Feedback Challenge

Materials: Soft ball, floor target, marked throwing line, tape measure or marked floor grid.
Time: About 15 minutes to learn and prepare, then setup, up to 30 minutes for data collection, and reflection.

Complete Learn & Prepare before opening Lab Setup.

1. Learn & Prepare

About 15 minutes · Work individually. Open the Canvas reference page Motor Learning, Feedback & Practice. Read the named sections, apply the ideas below, then check and revise your reasoning. Short, specific responses are enough.

1. Where does feedback come from? About 4 minutes

Read: In the reference page’s Feedback tab, read intrinsic and augmented feedback.

Check your response:

  • Did you identify information available through the learner’s own senses?
  • Did you distinguish that from information added by the coach?
  • Did you explain which information becomes unavailable when the learner closes their eyes?

Use the reference page to resolve any uncertainty, then revise your response if needed. These questions do not verify whether your answer is correct.

2. What does feedback communicate? About 6 minutes

Read: In Feedback, review KR versus KP, qualitative versus quantitative feedback, and the distinction between instruction and feedback.

Observed wall-target throw: The ball struck the wall approximately 30 centimeters above and 15 centimeters left of the marked target. The learner’s elbow moved outward during release.

Apply the reference page to this new situation. Use only the observations above to write what the coach would say in each condition.

Check your statements:

  • Does your qualitative KR describe the outcome without a numerical measurement?
  • Does your quantitative KR include an approximate measured outcome?
  • Does your combined statement include both an outcome and an observed movement detail?
  • Does the KP portion describe what happened rather than tell the learner what to do?
  • Did you stay within the supplied observations instead of inventing a cause?

Brief partner check: Explain one statement to a partner. Ask them to identify its feedback type and explain why. Discuss any disagreement using the reference page, then revise if needed.

3. What would count as learning? About 5 minutes

Read: In Learning & Performance, review the definitions and the acquisition, retention, and transfer example.

Check your reasoning:

  • Did you distinguish performance during this session from a lasting change in skill?
  • Did you consider what else changed besides the feedback?
  • Does your proposed retention check include a delay and the same task?
  • Did you specify whether the coach’s feedback would be available during that check, and why?

Use the definitions and example in the reference page to examine your conclusion, then revise if needed.

2. Lab Setup

Use the roles and procedure below after completing your learning preparation.

Roles: Learner throws; coach gives feedback; recorder tracks results and keeps the area clear. Rotate after each learner completes all three rounds. Each person submits their own data, reflection, and PDF.

Feedback vocabulary

KR, knowledge of results: information about the outcome, such as where the ball landed.

KP, knowledge of performance: information about the movement, such as release or arm path.

Qualitative: describes direction or quality without a measurement.

Quantitative: includes a number or measured amount.

KR and KP describe what feedback is about. Qualitative and quantitative describe how that information is expressed.

How the hidden-target experiment works

  1. The coach sets a hidden target. Keep its direction and distance unknown to the learner.
  2. Stand at the fixed throwing line. Throw underhand with your non-dominant hand. Keep your eyes closed or covered for all five throws in the round, including between throws.
  3. After each throw, the coach gives only the assigned feedback. The recorder records the result, retrieves the ball, and hands it directly to the learner. Never throw the ball back. Wait for the recorder to confirm the area is clear before throwing again.
  4. After throw five, wait for the recorder to conceal the target and landing area before opening your eyes. The coach moves the target to the next hidden location while keeping it concealed.
  5. The same learner completes all three conditions: five throws with qualitative KR, five with quantitative KR, then five with KR + KP. Open your eyes only between rounds, after the recorder’s all-clear. Rotate roles only after all 15 throws: learner → coach → recorder → learner.

Safety: Stay stationary while your eyes are closed. Keep the throwing area clear and open your eyes before moving. If you feel unsafe or disoriented, stop and open your eyes immediately. If closing your eyes is unsafe or inaccessible, use a barrier that hides the target or take a coach/recorder role.

Keep the target fixed within each five-throw round. Use the same three hidden target locations for each learner.

Each student completes their own report. Record your own throws unless your instructor has arranged an observer role.

3. Qualitative KR

Outcome feedback, no numbers. After each throw, the coach reports only direction: “short,” “long,” “left,” “right,” or a combination. If the ball lands in the target, say “hit.”

Keep the target fixed and your eyes closed for all 5 throws. The recorder hands the ball back between throws. After throw five, open your eyes only when the recorder confirms the target and landing area are concealed. Keep your roles for the next condition.

4. Quantitative KR

Outcome feedback with numbers. The coach describes direction and approximate distance: “two feet short,” “one foot right,” or “hit.” Use a tape measure or marked floor grid; report distances in the same unit throughout. Estimates should be labeled approximate.

Keep the same learner, coach, and recorder. Move the target to a new hidden location, then keep it fixed for 5 throws. Eyes stay closed throughout; hand the ball back between throws. Open eyes after throw five only when the target and landing area are concealed. Do not give movement advice.

5. KR + KP

Outcome plus movement feedback. After each throw, the coach gives a brief KR statement (where it landed) and one KP cue (what the throwing movement looked like).

KR example

“The ball landed short and left.”

KP example

“Your release was very low.”

Describe one observed movement; do not guess what caused a miss.

Keep the same roles. Move the target to a new hidden location, then keep it fixed for 5 throws. Eyes stay closed throughout; hand the ball back between throws. After throw five, wait for the recorder’s all-clear before opening your eyes. Now rotate roles and repeat all three conditions with the next learner. This round combines KR and KP; it does not test KP alone.
